# V.34

Checked against V.34 (02/1998). This is duplex operation on the switched
network (§ 11.1 to § 11.7), up to 33 600 bit/s. It does not do half-duplex
operation (§ 10.2, § 12), the two-wire leased line (§ 11.8), the control
channel of Annex A, or the auxiliary channel.

There is no reference for it but the ITU text. spandsp's V.34 does not get
through phase 2, so it is not used. See [scope](scope.md).

## Line signals

- The primary channel carries 2400 to 33 600 bit/s in steps of 2400 bit/s,
  synchronously (§ 5.1). Start-stop characters cross it as [V.14](v14.md)
  describes, or [V.42](v42.md) runs over it.
- The symbol rate is S = (a/c) · 2400 ± 0.01% (§ 5.2, table 1). 2400, 3000
  and 3200 are mandatory, 2743, 2800 and 3429 optional. Each direction has
  its own carrier and pre-emphasis. A different symbol rate each way is
  optional (§ 5.2, § 5.3).

  | S | a/c | Low carrier | High carrier |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2400 | 1/1 | 1600 Hz | 1800 Hz |
  | 2743 | 8/7 | 1646 Hz | 1829 Hz |
  | 2800 | 7/6 | 1680 Hz | 1867 Hz |
  | 3000 | 5/4 | 1800 Hz | 2000 Hz |
  | 3200 | 4/3 | 1829 Hz | 1920 Hz |
  | 3429 | 10/7 | 1959 Hz | 1959 Hz |

- The transmitter shapes its spectrum with one of 11 pre-emphasis filters
  that the far receiver picks (§ 5.4, tables 3 and 4).
- Each direction has its own self-synchronising scrambler: 1 + x⁻¹⁸ + x⁻²³
  from the call modem, 1 + x⁻⁵ + x⁻²³ from the answer modem (§ 7).
- A superframe is 280 ms: J = 7 or 8 data frames of P = 12 to 16 mapping
  frames, each of four 4D symbols, each of two 2D symbols (§ 8.1, table 7).
  A data frame carries N = R · 0.28/J bits. Mapping frames carry b or b − 1
  bits in the switching pattern SWP, which a counter generates (§ 8.2).

## The encoder

The encoder turns each mapping frame into eight 2D points (§ 9, figure 4).
Each step must match the text exactly, or the far receiver and precoder
drift apart.

1. **Parser** (§ 9.3). The first K bits go to the shell mapper. Each of the
   four 4D symbols then gets three bits I1, I2, I3, and each 2D symbol gets
   q more. At b ≤ 12, K = 0 and there is no shell mapping.
2. **Shell mapper** (§ 9.4). The K bits become eight ring indices below M,
   by the counting algorithm of equations 9-3 to 9-24. M is the minimum or
   the expanded value of table 10, as the far receiver asks. Expanded rings
   give shaping gain.
3. **Differential encoder** (§ 9.5). I2 and I3 of each 4D symbol are added
   modulo 4 to the previous quadrant.
4. **Mapper** (§ 9.6.1). The ring index and the q bits pick a point from the
   1664-point superconstellation of figure 5. The pair is turned by the
   quadrant, and the second point also by I1 and the trellis bit U0.
5. **Precoder** (§ 9.6.2). A three-tap filter with coefficients from the far
   receiver pre-distorts the points, so that its equaliser need not feed
   back decisions. With all coefficients 0 it does nothing.
6. **Trellis encoder** (§ 9.6.3). A 16-, 32- or 64-state systematic
   convolutional encoder over the subset labels of the channel output,
   picked by the far receiver. Bit inversions in U0 at each half data frame
   mark the superframe (table 12).
7. **Non-linear encoder** (§ 9.7). With Θ = 0.3125, larger points are
   pushed out, to spread the quantisation noise of a PCM codec more evenly.
   With Θ = 0 it does nothing.

## Start-up

Four phases (§ 11.1 to § 11.4). All times T are symbol periods.

**Phase 1** is [V.8](v8.md). The answer modem is silent for at least
200 ms, then sends ANSam with phase reversals. CM and JM offer V.34 duplex.
After CJ both ends are silent for 75 ± 5 ms.

**Phase 2**, probing and ranging (§ 11.2, figure 16):

- INFO sequences go as binary DPSK at 600 bit/s: a 1 turns the carrier by
  180° (§ 10.1.2.3.1). The answer modem sends at 2400 Hz, 1 dB below
  nominal, with an 1800 Hz guard tone 7 dB below. The call modem sends at
  1200 Hz. Each has fill bits, the frame sync 01110010, its fields, and a
  CRC of x¹⁶ + x¹² + x⁵ + 1 preset to ones (§ 10.1.2.3.2).
- INFO0 (49 bits, table 14) gives the symbol rates, carriers and
  constellation size a modem supports. Bit 28 acknowledges the far INFO0.
- Tone A is 2400 Hz from the answer modem, tone B 1200 Hz from the call
  modem. Their 180° phase reversals are timed to measure the round trip
  delay: each end answers a reversal 40 ± 1 ms after it arrives (§ 11.2.1).
- L1 and L2 are 21 tones 150 Hz apart, from 150 to 3750 Hz without 900,
  1200, 1800 and 2400, with the phases of table 17 (§ 10.1.2.4). L1 lasts
  160 ms at 6 dB above nominal, L2 up to 550 ms plus a round trip at
  nominal. From them the receiver measures the channel.
- INFO1c (109 bits, table 15) holds the call modem's results for each
  symbol rate: carrier, pre-emphasis and projected rate for the answer
  modem's transmitter, and the frequency offset. INFO1a (70 bits, table 16)
  gives the answer modem's results and picks the symbol rate each way.

**Phase 3**, equaliser and echo canceller training (§ 11.3, figure 19).
The ends take turns: while one sends, the other is silent, so each trains
its echo canceller on its own signal. Each sends S for 128T and S̄ for 16T,
the optional MD, S and S̄ again, PP for the equaliser, TRN for at least
512T, then J. J asks the far end to use 4 or 16 points in phase 4
(table 18).

**Phase 4**, final training (§ 11.4, figure 20). Both send TRN, then MP
sequences with the data rates, trellis encoder, Θ, shaping and precoding
coefficients each receiver wants (tables 20 and 21). MP′ acknowledges. After
E, 20 ones, each sends B1, one data frame of scrambled ones, and data
begins. With symmetric rates, each direction runs at the highest rate that
both modems enable and neither exceeds (§ 11.4.1.1.3).

## Retrain, rate renegotiation and cleardown

- A retrain (§ 11.5) goes back to phase 2. The call modem sends tone B, the
  answer modem tone A, after 70 ± 5 ms of silence. An end that hears the
  other's tone for more than 50 ms joins it.
- A rate renegotiation (§ 11.6) is S, S̄, TRN and MP from both ends, with
  4-point TRN, MP and E, then B1 at the new rate.
- A cleardown (§ 11.7) is a rate renegotiation that asks for 0 bit/s each
  way. An end that sends and receives MP′ ends the call.
- Each recovery rule of § 11.2.2 to § 11.4.2 has a timeout in terms of the
  round trip delay. An end that loses its way sends INFOMARKS, all ones, or
  starts a retrain.

## This modem, in these terms

- It offers all six symbol rates and both carriers, and picks the fastest
  that line probing supports. Between two instances the path passes up to
  4 kHz. Behind an ATA the codec's filters may cut the top of 3429's band,
  and then the probing tones above 3400 Hz arrive weak and 3200 wins.
- It offers 1664-point constellations, and so rates above 28 800 bit/s.
- A rate needs 4 dB + 3.01 dB per bit per 2D symbol, where the 16-state
  Viterbi decoder reaches an error rate of 10⁻⁶ in white noise.
- Line probing picks the symbol rate, with 5 dB of margin, as the probing
  SNR is only an estimate. The data rate that MP asks for in the direction
  it hears comes from the equaliser's error on the far phase 4 TRN, with
  2 dB of margin. It listens to 1536T of that TRN, as the equaliser and the
  slow timing loop settle over the first 512T. A far end that sends MP
  after its least 512T ends the measurement early.
- The A-law hop in the path adds quantisation noise at about 38 dB below
  the signal, at any level, as the companding is logarithmic. μ-law is no
  better. 33 600 bit/s at 3429 baud needs 33.5 dB, so this leaves over
  4 dB, and 2 dB of margin.
- Rates are symmetric: INFO0 allows no difference in symbol rate, and MP
  bit 50 is 0. There is no auxiliary channel and no MD. It does not reduce
  its transmit power.
- Its transmitter does everything a far receiver may ask for: all three
  trellis encoders, both values of Θ, both shapings, all 11 pre-emphasis
  filters, and precoding.
- For Θ, the average energy of § 9.7 is taken as the mean energy of the
  points the shell mapper picks, which the text does not pin down.
- Its receiver asks for the 16-state encoder, Θ = 0, minimum shaping, and
  no precoding, as MP type 0. That costs some dB against a receiver that
  asks for more, and keeps the Viterbi decoder and the equaliser simple.
- The guard tone goes 7 dB below nominal with tone A too. § 10.1.2.1 puts it
  at nominal, which with tone A at 1 dB below exceeds the nominal power by
  2.5 dB. § 10.1.2.3.1 has 7 dB below for INFO.
- V.8 offers V.34 duplex in modn0, and V.34 is the highest modulation of
  automode. `+MS=V34` selects it.

## The pump

The transmitter is the encoder above, then V.22's pulse shaping and
modulation at the symbol rate. At 8000 samples/s no symbol rate is a whole
number of samples, so symbol timing is a fractional accumulator, as V.21's
bits are. The pulse is a square root raised cosine with 10% roll-off over
12 symbols each side, flat to ±0.45 S around the carrier as figures 1 and
2 ask. Pre-emphasis is a 31-tap filter that follows the template, with a
straight line across the 0.4 to 0.8 that figure 2 leaves open, scaled so
that the power in the band does not change.

The receiver:

- Phase 2 needs a DPSK demodulator at 600 bit/s for each INFO carrier,
  tone detectors for A and B that time a phase reversal to within 1 ms, and
  a spectrum of L1 and L2. 8000 samples/s is 160 samples per three periods
  of L1, so a 160-point DFT has a bin on each probing tone.
- From phase 3 on it mixes to baseband and evaluates the matched filter at
  two points a symbol, from a table of the pulse at 256 phases a sample.
  Gardner timing recovery follows the far clock quickly through phase 3,
  and slowly from the phase 4 TRN on, where its self-noise would limit the
  SNR.
- A fractionally spaced equaliser, trained on PP and TRN and then adapted
  by decisions, feeds a phase locked loop. The loop weighs its phase error
  by the power of the point, as the inner points of a large constellation
  would jitter it.
- Data then goes through a 16-state Viterbi decoder, differential decoding,
  the inverse of the shell mapper, and the descrambler. The bit inversions
  of table 12 give the superframe.
- An echo canceller removes its own signal reflected at the far hybrid.
  Between two instances there is none, as the path has no hybrid. Against a
  real modem behind an ATA there is, in the same band as the far signal.

## Testing

Without a reference, each part is checked against the text and against its
own inverse: the shell mapper against its demapper, the trellis encoder
against the Viterbi decoder, INFO and MP against their parser, and the
whole pump between two instances.

The test channel adds what the real path adds: A-law quantisation, noise,
gain error, a clock offset between the two ends, packet loss, and an echo of
the transmitter at a delay, as a hybrid would return it.
