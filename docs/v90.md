# V.90

Checked against V.90 (09/1998). The digital modem sends PCM codewords down
at up to 56 000 bit/s, and the analogue modem sends V.34 up at up to
33 600 bit/s. This modem is both: the call modem is the analogue modem, and
the answer modem the digital modem (§ 9.1.1). See "56k" in
[scope](scope.md) for why that works over A-law RTP.

slmodemd, as the analogue modem, checks the digital side. See
[interop](interop.md).

## Line signals

- Downstream, the digital modem sends one PCM codeword for each sample, at
  8000 symbols/s (§ 5.2). A codeword is a Ucode of table 1 and a sign. The
  pump gives the linear value of table 1, and `alaw::encode` turns it back
  into the same octet. Ucode 0 is the nearest to silence, as A-law has no
  zero.
- Six symbols make a data frame, and each position in it, a data frame
  interval, has its own constellation (§ 5.4). The first symbol of Sd is
  interval 0, and every signal after it is a whole number of frames.
- Upstream is V.34 at 3000, 3200 or 3429 baud (§ 6.2), with its own carrier
  and pre-emphasis from INFO1d.
- The digital modem scrambles with GPC, the V.34 call modem's polynomial,
  and the analogue modem with GPA. In phase 2 the digital modem takes the
  part of the V.34 call modem, at 1200 Hz with tone B, and the analogue
  modem the part of the answer modem. So in V.90 terms the answerer does
  what a V.34 caller does, and the other way round.

## The encoder

The digital modem's encoder turns D bits into one data frame (§ 5.4,
figure 1). D is drn + 20 in data mode and drn + 8 in phase 4. The rate,
D · 8000/6 bit/s, is not a whole number, so D is taken from drn and not from
the rate.

1. **Parser** (§ 5.4.2). The first S bits are sign bits, the next K go to
   the modulus encoder. S = 6 − Sr.
2. **Modulus encoder** (§ 5.4.3). K bits as a number, divided by M0 to M5 in
   turn. The remainders pick a label in each interval.
3. **Mapper** (§ 5.4.4). Label 0 is the largest Ucode of the interval's
   constellation.
4. **Sign bits** (§ 5.4.5). With Sr = 0, a differential encoding across the
   frame. With Sr of 1 to 3, spectral shaping frames of 6, 3 or 2 symbols: a
   differential encoding of the odd bits (table 4), a second one from frame
   to frame, and one of the rules A to D of figure 2 for each frame.
5. **Spectral shaper** (§ 5.4.5.5). The rule for a frame is the one that
   keeps the metric w[n] of § 5.4.5.6 lowest over that frame and the ld
   frames after it. This modem tries every allowed sequence, which is 16 at
   most, as ld is 3 at most.

The receiver needs no trellis to undo the shaping. The rules of figure 2
flip the even and the odd sign bits of a frame in a way that the state
before and after it fixes. So the difference of two frames' sign bits
cancels the rules, and the two differential encodings recover the bits from
it. The encoder and the decoder are checked against each other for every Sr
and ld.

## Start-up

**Phase 1** is [V.8](v8.md). CM has `pcm0` with the analogue bit, JM has it
with the digital bit, and both have `access0` with digital access, as this
modem always sits on RTP.

**Phase 2** is phase 2 of [V.34](v34.md) with the roles above. INFO0d has
the V.34 fields, the power and the PCM coding. INFO0a has the layout of the
V.34 INFO0, and INFO1d that of INFO1c. The analogue modem's INFO1a picks
the upstream symbol rate and UINFO, the Ucode that the digital modem
trains with.

**Phase 3** (§ 9.3, figure 5). The analogue modem sends S, S̄, PP and TRN,
then Ja, the DIL descriptor, over and over. The digital modem answers with
Sd and S̄d, TRN1d on UINFO, and Jd until the analogue modem sends S. Then
Jd′, and the DIL that Ja asked for, until the second S̄ from the analogue
modem. Through DIL the analogue modem sends SCR, which § 9.3.2.9 allows in
place of silence, so that DIL meets the path as data mode will. On a real
call through a VoIP provider, the path added far more noise in data mode
than during a silent DIL.

**Phase 4** (§ 9.4, figure 7). The digital modem sends Ri until CPt, then
R̄i and TRN2d with the constellations of CPt, then MP until CP and MP′
until CP′ or E. Ed, then B1d with the constellations of CP. The analogue
modem sends CPt, SCR, CP and CP′, E and B1.

## This modem, in these terms

- Its INFO0d gives -13 dBm0 in phase 2, a highest power of -12 dBm0 (the
  limit in the United States), the power at the codec, A-law and 3429 baud
  upstream.
- As the analogue modem, it asks for UINFO at the nominal -13 dBm0, which
  is Ucode 75.
- As the analogue modem, it ignores bits 26:27 of INFO0d, as Table 7 says.
  A V.92 digital modem sets them to offer short phase 2 and V.92
  (§ 8.4.1/V.92), and they are not the V.34 transmit clock there.
- As the digital modem, its Jd offers every rate from 28 000 to 56 000
  bit/s, CP on 4 points, and a look-ahead of 3. Its receiver also reads CP
  on 16 points, and trains its equaliser only on symbols near one of them,
  as S and S̄ before CP lie between them.
- As the analogue modem, it sends CP′ until Ed comes, or for 200 ms, and
  only then E. § 9.4.2.4 asks for one CP′, but one CP′ and E fit in one
  RTP packet, and with that packet lost the digital modem sends MP′ for
  ever. For the same reason it sends S and S̄ again if no Ri comes within a
  round trip and 500 ms of its S̄, as a lost S̄ leaves the digital modem in
  DIL (§ 9.3.1.6).
- As the analogue modem, it sends CPt, CP and E on the 4 or 16 points that
  bit 47 of Jd asks for in phase 4, and bit 48 in a renegotiation (§ 8.5.2).
  Digital modems in the field ask for 16 in phase 4.
- It sends Sd as soon as it has one Ja, TRN1d for 2040T, and TRN2d for
  2040T.
- Its MP asks the analogue modem for the rate that the error of its
  equaliser at the end of phase 3 allows, the 16-state trellis, Θ = 0,
  minimum shaping and no precoding, unless it is built to ask for more
  (`Digital::asking`). Its upstream decoder is V.34's, which undoes all of
  them.
- A stray symbol between two signals moves every data frame after it, and
  the far end then misses R̄i. The transmitter refills its queue until it
  has a symbol.
- slmodemd, the analogue modem it has been checked against, recovers the
  PCM symbol clock from what arrives at 4000 Hz. A line card's D/A leaves
  some there, so the bridge to slmodemd lets it through.

## The analogue modem's receiver

The path to the analogue modem is digital, so each sample is one symbol,
and there is no equaliser and no timing recovery.

- It finds Sd by its shape: four large symbols, two small ones, and the
  signs of + + + − − −. S̄d fixes the frame alignment. Neither depends on
  the gain of the path.
- It measures the gain on TRN1d, and reads Jd from the signs. Jd′ is the
  16 zeros that follow a whole Jd: its fill, then Jd′ itself.
- Its DIL asks for 112 segments of 240 symbols, one for each Ucode from 0
  to 111: from UINFO up, then from below UINFO down. Each segment
  alternates a frame of its Ucode with a frame of UINFO, the reference
  (TP). The signs are a pseudo-random SP of 120 bits, in which each second
  training frame inverts the first, so the DIL has no strong tones and each
  data frame interval gets each sign of its Ucode ten times. The Ucodes
  from 112 up are left out, as CP can hardly use them within table 15, and
  CP never picks them. It lasts about 3.4 s, and from that one pass the
  modem learns the level of each Ucode in each interval, and the noise of
  each Uchord from how far the symbols stray from those levels. A Uchord
  that shows nothing takes the noise of the noisiest.
- The reference comes all through DIL, so it shows a path that changes
  while DIL lasts. Its noise, in steps of its Uchord, bounds the noise of
  every Uchord from below, in steps of that Uchord. On real calls through a
  VoIP provider the path changed halfway through a DIL, and Ucodes came one
  step off from there on, which the Ucodes' own segments before the change
  could not show. slmodemd's DIL, which asks for a reference in half of
  each segment too, gave the idea.
- It picks each level in CP from the bottom, as widely as table 15 allows,
  at the highest rate that fits. Each two neighbouring levels are at least
  8 apart, and at least 5.5 times the noise of each one's Uchord. So a path
  that adds more noise to some levels than to others spaces those alone.
  On a path that changes nothing that is 64 levels and 56 000 bit/s.
  Behind a pad, Ucodes that the pad merges have one level, and the spacing
  leaves them out.
- CPt asks for 32 000 bit/s on 8 levels, or less, down to 16 000 bit/s on
  2, where the noise leaves no room for 8 within the power limit. Where
  even the lowest rate is too fast for the spacing above, CPt and CP take
  it with the levels as far apart as the power limit allows. Neither CPt
  nor CP asks for spectral shaping, as no D/A follows.
- It slices each symbol to the nearest learned level of its interval's
  constellation. Ed is two data frames of zeros after MP, and the frame
  after it is B1d, with the constellations of CP.

## Retrains, rate renegotiation and cleardown

- A retrain (§ 9.5) runs phase 2 again from the tones, with the far INFO0a
  or INFO0d kept, and then phases 3 and 4. The end that starts it sends
  70 ms of silence and then its tone. The other end answers when it hears
  that tone for more than 50 ms in data mode, and the analogue modem also
  in phases 3 and 4 (§ 9.3.2, § 9.4.2), where a digital modem that gets
  nothing it can use retrains. Its tone detector gave no false start on
  the phase 3 and 4 signals of real calls. Sd and S̄d give the data frames
  again. ATO1 retrains, as with V.34.

- A rate renegotiation (§ 9.6) is Rd and R̄d, then MP, from the digital
  modem, and S and S̄, then CP, from the analogue modem, whichever starts
  it. Then MP′, CP′, Ed, E, B1d and B1, as at the end of phase 4. The
  digital modem hears S and S̄ in data mode, and the analogue modem Rd, on
  the largest codeword of each interval, and R̄d.
- The analogue modem looks for Rd at every sample, not only at the data
  frame boundary it knows. Samples lost with no gap to fill move the data
  frames, and after that every frame decodes wrong. R̄d then gives the
  boundary again.
- Rd and R̄d begin on a data frame boundary. The digital modem sends no
  TRN2d before MP, which § 9.6.1.1.1 leaves optional. MP and Ed use the
  constellations and K of CPt with the spectral shaping of data mode
  (§ 8.6).
- An end that starts a renegotiation forgets the far MP or CP of data mode,
  so that only a new one ends its own. It goes on hearing data until the far
  S or Rd, as the far end sends data until then.
- CPs asks for silence (§ 9.6.2.1.3). The digital modem then sends MP′
  until CPs′, Ed, and Ucode 0 in whole data frames. On the next CP it sends
  Rt and R̄t, on the largest codewords of CPt, and goes on from MP.
  slmodemd asks for this when noise reaches it. This modem, as the
  analogue modem, never asks for it.
- A cleardown (§ 9.7) is a renegotiation whose MP or CP asks for 0 bit/s.
  After the acknowledgements both ends are silent and the call is over. ATH
  clears down this way, as with V.34.
- DCD and the bit rate hold through a retrain or a renegotiation.
- As the analogue modem, it watches its margin in data mode. Each second
  it measures how far the symbols stray from the levels DIL showed, and
  puts the larger of that and DIL's noise into the design of CP above. If
  that gives a lower rate than the one it runs, it renegotiates with the
  new CP. It judges nothing in the first 2 s of data mode. It leaves out
  the worst 5 of each second's 50 blocks of 27 frames, as one lost RTP
  packet filled with silence would otherwise count as noise and take the
  rate from 56 000 down to 33 333 bit/s for the rest of the call. Where the
  symbols of a Uchord stray past 15% of the gap to the next level, many
  land nearer another level and so stray less than the path makes them;
  the gap then counts as their noise. As a safety net it is conservative:
  the worst noise it measures counts for every Uchord. It moves the rate
  only down.
- The analogue modem holds data mode only once four frames of Rd have
  come, the number a renegotiation needs before R̄d. At the lowest rates
  data looks like one frame of Rd several times a minute.
- This modem also starts a renegotiation, and then a retrain, on its own
  when LAPM hears nothing sound (see [V.42](v42.md)).
- Over RTP one lost packet can take a signal that is sent once, so it
  recovers from each such loss it has met:
  - In phase 2, a lost INFO0a or INFO0d as § 9.2.1.2.1 and § 9.2.2.2.1
    say. As the analogue modem it reverses tone A again after 2 s with no
    reply (§ 9.2.2.2.2), and retrains if INFO1d does not come within 2 s
    and two round trips (§ 9.2.2.2.4), as in [V.34](v34.md).
  - As the analogue modem, it retrains if Sd and S̄d do not come within
    1.5 s and a round trip of the start of Ja (§ 9.3.2.4), and if Ed and
    B1d do not come within 1 s and a round trip of its E, as V.90 allows a
    retrain at any time in phase 4.
  - As the digital modem, it answers the analogue modem's retrain from
    phase 3 on (§ 9.3.1, § 9.4.1).
  A lost E, which cannot come twice, leaves the digital modem to retrain.

## Testing

Each sequence is checked against its own reader, and the encoder against
the decoder. The analogue side is checked against the digital side over
A-law, behind a pad of -3 dB, and over lines delayed by amounts that are
not whole data frames. Both renegotiate and clear down from either end. Two
modems connect with `+MS=V90` at 56 000 bit/s, and
`checks.aarch64-linux.ppp-v90` runs PPP over it.

They are also checked on a VoIP provider's path as real calls met it (see
[transport](transport.md)): a round trip of about 0.3 s, frames concealed
as silence, and a gateway that adds noise toward the analogue modem once
the analogue modem has been quiet. Seeds that lose INFO1d, Sd or Ed must
connect and carry data, and so must any one lost upstream frame of phase 4
before E. `ppp-v90-voip` runs PPP over such a path, and connects at
29 333 bit/s, which the margin monitor takes down to 28 000.

The digital side is checked against slmodemd:
`checks.aarch64-linux.slmodemd-v90` connects at 54 667 bit/s down and
31 200 bit/s up, passes text both ways, and clears down on ATH.
`slmodemd-v90-renegotiate` adds noise toward slmodemd once in data mode.
`slmodemd-v90-voip` puts slmodemd on the VoIP provider's path above, and
it connects at 37 333 bit/s.
slmodemd renegotiates with silence, down to 52 000 bit/s, and text still
passes. slmodemd's V.90 takes several times the CPU of its V.34, and the
guest needs four CPUs to keep real time emulated. With one, the calls
fail in a different place each time.
